## Crude Oil (WTI / Brent) — Leaning bearish (-1)

**What's happening.** WTI recovered from a 4.25-month low and closed modestly
higher (+0.16%) as short covering emerged on a two-week-low dollar, and prices
are now stuck in a tight range while traders await clarity on the fragile
US-Iran ceasefire ([Barchart via CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/oil/),
[OilPrice](https://oilprice.com/)). The supply overhang persists: Saudi exports
are back to 6.3M bbl/d (90% of pre-war levels), UAE shipments have nearly fully
recovered, and Iran has exported 40M barrels in two weeks since the end of the
US blockade, selling at a premium.

**What it means.** The glut narrative hasn't changed, but the market has
stopped falling on it — that's a sentiment floor forming, hence the upgrade
from bearish to leaning bearish. The two-sided risk now is the ceasefire:
any wobble puts a bid back in fast, while confirmation of durable peace
plus Iranian barrels keeps the pressure on. Watch EIA inventories and any
OPEC+ signal about defending prices.

that away. Thursday's EIA print and any July heat-dome revival are the next
catalysts; EIA still sees H2 averaging ~$3.34, so the downside from here is
also limited.

## Wheat — Bullish (+2)

**What's happening.** Wheat firmed to ~$5.99-6.08/bu, its highest since
June 22, after USDA reported June 1 stocks of 920M bushels (below
expectations) and all-wheat plantings of 42.74M acres (under forecasts)

**What it means.** Still the board's most durable bull story — the stocks and
acreage misses added fresh fuel to the multi-decade supply narrative. But the
Black Sea counterweight is real: if Russian/Ukrainian export offers stay
aggressive, US futures do the work of rationing alone. Harvest-pace updates
and export sales are the next tells; +3 would require a Black Sea crop problem.

next two weeks remains the swing factor; a cool, wet forecast run is the main
thing that fades this.
