## Crude Oil (WTI / Brent) — Bearish (-2)

**What's happening.** WTI fell for a third straight session to roughly $67-69,
back to levels last seen before the US-Israel-Iran conflict began, as maritime
flows through the Strait of Hormuz rapidly recovered
([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/7/2/oil-prices-fall-to-levels-not-seen-since-start-of-us-israel-war-on-iran),
[Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/crude-oil)). The UAE
has restored exports above 3.9M bbl/d, total Hormuz flows have passed 10M bbl/d,
and emergency reserve releases plus ad hoc Saudi sales to Asia have tipped the
market into surplus. Analysts (Pickering Energy) say the market is trading
sentiment more than fundamentals and expect prices to grind lower until stranded
barrels clear.

**What it means.** The war-risk premium is essentially gone, and the burden of
proof has flipped to bulls: the story to chase now is how fast the surplus
clears and whether OPEC+ responds to defend prices. A headline that reverses
sentiment would be a fresh disruption to Gulf flows or a surprise draw in EIA
inventories; absent that, expect "oversold" chatter to build.

**What it means.** Supply is winning the narrative — record production plus
above-normal injections outweigh episodic heat. The near-term swing factor is
whether next week's heat wave materializes strongly enough to shrink the
surplus; watch Thursday's EIA storage print and CDD forecast revisions before
the next edition.

**What it means.** This is a macro-beta bounce, not (yet) an industrial-demand
story — silver is simply higher-beta gold right now. If Fed repricing holds,
silver typically outruns gold on the way up; a hot inflation print would
unwind it just as fast. Watch the gold/silver ratio for confirmation.

## Wheat — Bullish (+2)

**What's happening.** Winter wheat led a grain rally, with Chicago SRW up
11.25¢ to ~$5.92 and KC HRW up to ~$6.23, on technical buying spurred by a
record heatwave over the central US
([Farm Progress](https://www.farmprogress.com/markets-and-quotes/afternoon-market-recap),

**What it means.** This is the most one-sidedly bullish setup on the board —
a genuine multi-decade supply story, not just a weather blip. The risks to the
narrative are demand rationing and Black Sea export pressure undercutting US
prices; watch weekly export sales for whether high prices are already choking
demand.

**What it means.** Bullish inputs are stacking (tighter balance sheet + heat

weather over the next two weeks will decide whether this becomes a full
weather-market rally or fades on the first cooler forecast run.

**What it means.** Beans are the odd one out of the grain rally: the balance
sheet is loosening while demand — especially Chinese buying — is absent. The
same heat threatening corn could yet lift beans in August (their key yield
month), but until export sales revive, rallies are likely to be sold. Watch
for any China purchase announcements and the next USDA export sales report.
